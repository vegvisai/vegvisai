# Capitalism on Rails

Why we build VegvisAI, and what we think matters for businesses as customers start asking AI instead of searching.

## Worried about AI? You have reason to be careful

Customers are starting to ask an AI assistant where to buy, book or get help. For many businesses that feels like losing control: a new layer between you and your customer, built by companies you have never met, with rules you cannot see.

The worry is fair. Every new channel tempts someone to put up a toll booth: pay to be shown, pay to rank, pay to stay visible. We think there is a better way, and that it is in your hands more than it seems.

## Step one: make buying easy, on your own site

An AI can only help a customer buy from you if the facts are clear and the next step is simple. That means:

- prices, availability, opening hours and terms written plainly on your own pages
- links that open a filled-in order, booking or request form, so the customer only has to confirm
- payment and the customer relationship on your own platform, not someone else's

Open standards for this already exist, from schema.org to commerce protocols such as ACP, UCP and MCP. Used well, they make the transaction work for you, on your terms.

## Then it is about who you are

When buying is easy everywhere, the transaction stops being the difference. What remains is how you present yourself and what you offer: honest facts, clear terms, the things you do well and the things you don't do, your story and your tone. That is what builds trust, and trust is what makes an assistant, and a customer, choose you again.

## AI-compatible, with your signature

Everything you publish should be readable by every AI, big or small. But the voice, the offer and the relationship must stay yours. We do not squeeze businesses into one template that makes everyone look the same, and we never take over the customer relationship. The signature on the work is always the business's own.

## Healthy competition

We believe in competition where the customer is satisfied, the business earns money, and the market stays sustainable. The best business should win the customer because it is the best, not because it paid the most to be at the top. We are here to help with that: an open toolkit anyone can use for free, and paid help for those who want it.

**Kapitalisme på skinner.** In Norwegian, «på skinner» means both «on rails» and «running as it should». Free competition on fair tracks that no one can buy their way past.

## The manifesto

We like competition. Real competition, where the best business gets the customer because it is the best, not because it paid the most to be at the top. Now customers are starting to ask their AI instead of searching themselves. That is a chance to do it right from the start, or to build even more toll booths. We choose the first.

1.

**The road to the customer should not be owned by anyone.** Not by us either.
2.

**The business owns its data, customers and till.** We link and index. We keep only what businesses publish themselves, and we never sell it.
3.

**Ranking can't be bought.** The criteria are open. Early users get better terms, never a better position.
4.

**Open to every AI,** from the largest assistants to the model running on your laptop.
5.

**Rights before ads.** If the customer is entitled to free help, from a public service or under the law, we say so before we show a single offer.
6.

**The small business should be as easy to find as the chain.** The bakery in Bodø should not need a marketing budget to be found.
7.

**We don't reinvent the wheel. We lay the rails.** Open standards: schema.org, AI Catalog, `llms.txt` and MCP.
8.

**Not greedy.** We charge for work, such as setup, hosting and training, and never for access.
9.

**Public services are free,** and they are never ranked against businesses.
10.

**If we ever become a gatekeeper, fork us.** The code and the index are open precisely so that this is possible.

### What we don't do

- No «disruption». We just want the customer to find you.
- No walled garden with a kiosk at the gate.
- No blitzscaling, and no investors who need 100x.
- We don't intend to «own the customer relationship». It is yours.
- We are not against big companies. We are against toll booths.

The title is a nod to Ruby on Rails and David Heinemeier Hansson, who has shown that open source and profitable, non-greedy business go together. No affiliation.
